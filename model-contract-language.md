# Model Contract Language: Surveillance Footage Integrity Requirements

This document provides clause-ready language for cities and counties negotiating or renewing contracts with Flock Safety or comparable license plate reader and surveillance camera vendors. Each clause is followed by commentary explaining what it requires and why.

This language is designed to be inserted into a standard municipal services agreement as a dedicated section titled **"Data Integrity and Evidence Authentication Standards"** or equivalent. Defined terms in this section supplement but do not replace defined terms elsewhere in the agreement.

---

## Defined Terms

**"Audit Log"** means the complete, chronological record of all system events associated with Footage, including but not limited to capture, transmission, storage, access, query, export, transfer, and deletion, maintained in the manner specified in Section 3 of this Addendum.

**"External Immutable Anchor"** means a cryptographic commitment to a Merkle Root published to a content-addressed, permanently stored, publicly verifiable external system, including without limitation Arweave or a functionally equivalent system that provides permanent, tamper-evident, independently verifiable storage outside Vendor's infrastructure.

**"Footage"** means any video, image, metadata, timestamp, geolocation data, or associated information captured by any Camera operated under this Agreement.

**"Hardware Attestation"** means a cryptographic proof, generated by hardware security components within a Camera, that the Camera is operating expected firmware and that a specified hash value was computed within that hardware environment at a specified time.

**"Merkle Chain"** means a data structure in which each entry incorporates a cryptographic hash of its own content and the hash of the immediately preceding entry, such that any alteration of any entry is detectable by recomputing the chain from any subsequent entry.

**"Merkle Root"** means the single cryptographic hash value that represents the state of a complete Merkle Chain at a given point in time, from which the integrity of all entries in the chain can be independently verified.

**"Undetectable Alteration Capability"** means any technology, system, or method capable of modifying Footage or Audit Log entries in a manner that does not produce a detectable, permanent record in the Merkle-chained Audit Log specified in Section 3 of this Addendum.

**"WORM Storage"** means write-once, read-many storage in which entries can be appended but existing entries cannot be modified or deleted by any party, including Vendor.

## Section 1: Cryptographic Integrity at Point of Capture

**1.1** Vendor shall compute a cryptographic hash of each unit of Footage — defined as a video segment not exceeding [60] seconds in duration, or such shorter interval as the parties may agree — using SHA-256 or a stronger algorithm, within the Camera hardware that captured the Footage, before transmission of the Footage to any network, intermediate storage, or Vendor infrastructure.

**1.2** The hash computation required by Section 1.1 shall be performed within a hardware security element, trusted execution environment, or equivalent hardware-isolated computing environment within the Camera, such that the hash is generated from the original sensor data before any software processing, compression, or transmission.

**1.3** Vendor shall maintain Hardware Attestation records demonstrating that the hash computation required by Section 1.1 was performed within the Camera hardware environment and not by any software process operating after transmission.

**1.4** Vendor shall make Hardware Attestation records available to City upon request and to any party with a legal right to access Footage associated with a specific incident, including defense counsel in criminal proceedings in which the Footage is offered as evidence.

*Commentary: This is the most important clause in this document. If footage is not hashed at the sensor before it leaves the camera, any subsequent alteration — including alteration by a system operating on Vendor's network — cannot be detected by hash comparison. The hash must be computed inside the camera hardware, from the original sensor data, before anything else touches it. Hardware Attestation is what allows a court to verify that the hash actually came from the camera and not from a process that could itself have been compromised.*

---

## Section 2: Merkle Chaining

**2.1** Vendor shall organize all hashes computed under Section 1.1 into a continuous Merkle Chain covering all Footage captured by all Cameras operating under this Agreement.

**2.2** The Merkle Chain shall be structured such that any alteration, deletion, or insertion affecting any Footage hash is detectable by recomputing the chain from any subsequent entry. No entry in the Merkle Chain shall be modifiable or deletable after it has been appended.

**2.3** Vendor shall compute a Merkle Root at intervals not exceeding [one hour] and shall publish each Merkle Root to an External Immutable Anchor within [four hours] of computation.

**2.4** Vendor shall maintain a mapping between each External Immutable Anchor transaction identifier and the range of Footage covered by the corresponding Merkle Root, such that any specific unit of Footage can be located within the chain and its integrity verified against the published Merkle Root.

**2.5** Vendor shall provide City with access to the complete Merkle Chain, all Merkle Root values, and all External Immutable Anchor transaction identifiers upon request and shall maintain this access for the duration of the Agreement and for [seven] years following termination.

*Commentary: Merkle chaining is what makes integrity verification mathematical rather than trust-based. Without it, you have to trust Vendor's assertion that footage is unchanged. With it, anyone with the chain and the Merkle Root can verify independently. The External Immutable Anchor is critical because it puts the Merkle Root somewhere Vendor cannot reach and alter it — Arweave transactions, once confirmed, are permanent and publicly verifiable by anyone with the transaction ID.*

## Section 3: Append-Only Audit Logs

**3.1** Vendor shall maintain an Audit Log recording all system events associated with Footage captured under this Agreement, including without limitation:

- (a) Initial capture, including Camera identifier, timestamp, and hash value computed under Section 1.1
- (b) All transmissions of Footage, including source, destination, timestamp, and identity of the initiating process or user
- (c) All queries against Footage or associated metadata, including the querying agency, officer or user identifier, timestamp, and any case number or stated justification provided
- (d) All exports of Footage to any party, including law enforcement agencies, prosecutorial offices, and federal agencies
- (e) All transfers of access credentials or permissions
- (f) All deletions or purges of Footage, including the justification and the identity of the authorizing party
- (g) All administrative actions affecting the integrity controls described in this Addendum

**3.2** The Audit Log shall be maintained in WORM Storage. No entry in the Audit Log shall be modifiable or deletable after it has been written by any party, including Vendor, its employees, contractors, or agents.

**3.3** Audit Log entries shall be incorporated into the Merkle Chain specified in Section 2 alongside Footage hashes, such that Audit Log integrity is verifiable by the same mathematical process as Footage integrity.

**3.4** Vendor shall provide City with complete Audit Log records associated with any specific Camera, time period, or incident upon request within [five] business days.

**3.5** Vendor shall provide Audit Log records, in their original format with complete metadata, to defense counsel in any criminal proceeding in which Footage from a Camera operated under this Agreement is offered as evidence, upon receipt of a valid legal process or written authorization from the prosecuting agency.

*Commentary: Case numbers are not filled in on the majority of Flock searches, as the FOIA data shows. This clause requires logging regardless of whether a case number is provided. It also requires that federal agency access be logged with the same specificity as local agency access. The WORM requirement means Vendor cannot clean up the logs after a problematic access event.*

---

## Section 4: Prohibition on Undetectable Alteration Integrations

**4.1** Vendor shall not integrate with, provide data access to, or share Footage or Audit Log data with any system, service, or third party that possesses or operates an Undetectable Alteration Capability.

**4.2** Vendor shall conduct due diligence on all third parties with access to Footage or Audit Log data to verify that no such third party operates an Undetectable Alteration Capability, and shall provide City with documentation of that due diligence upon request.

**4.3** Vendor shall provide City with written notice within [48] hours of becoming aware of any integration, data access arrangement, or data transfer that may involve a party with an Undetectable Alteration Capability.

**4.4** A breach of this Section shall constitute a material breach of this Agreement and shall entitle City to immediate termination without penalty.

*Commentary: This clause is the direct response to the Toka issue. It requires Vendor to affirmatively verify that their integration partners cannot silently alter footage. It also creates a contractual record: if Vendor signs this clause and the footage is later found to have been altered without a detectable trace, Vendor has breached the contract and City has recourse. The 48-hour notice requirement and material breach designation give the clause teeth.*

---

## Section 5: Independent Audit Rights

**5.1** City shall have the right, upon [30] days' written notice, to conduct or commission an independent technical audit of Vendor's compliance with this Addendum, including without limitation:

- (a) Verification that hash computation is performed within Camera hardware as required by Section 1
- (b) Verification of the completeness and integrity of the Merkle Chain required by Section 2
- (c) Verification that External Immutable Anchors have been published on the schedule required by Section 2.3
- (d) Verification that Audit Logs are maintained in WORM Storage as required by Section 3
- (e) Spot verification that specific units of Footage can be authenticated against published Merkle Roots

**5.2** Vendor shall cooperate fully with audits conducted under Section 5.1, including by providing access to technical documentation, system logs, and personnel with knowledge of the integrity controls described in this Addendum.

**5.3** City may share audit findings with other jurisdictions operating under comparable agreements with Vendor, with defense counsel in proceedings involving Footage produced under this Agreement, and with law enforcement oversight bodies.

**5.4** Vendor shall provide City with a written certification of compliance with this Addendum on an annual basis, signed by a Vendor officer with knowledge of the integrity controls, and shall make that certification available to any court in which Footage produced under this Agreement is offered as evidence.

*Commentary: Audit rights without teeth are decorative. This clause gives City the ability to independently verify every commitment Vendor has made in this Addendum, and gives the audit findings legal utility by allowing them to be shared with courts and defense counsel. The annual certification creates a document Vendor has to stand behind in litigation.*

---

## Section 6: Consequences of Non-Compliance

**6.1** If City determines, based on an audit conducted under Section 5 or otherwise, that Vendor has failed to implement or maintain any control required by this Addendum, Vendor shall have [30] days to remediate the failure and provide City with written evidence of remediation.

**6.2** If Vendor fails to remediate within the period specified in Section 6.1, City may:

- (a) Terminate this Agreement without penalty upon [10] days' written notice
- (b) Withhold payment of fees accruing after the date of identified non-compliance until remediation is confirmed
- (c) Notify all agencies within City's jurisdiction that Footage produced under this Agreement during the period of non-compliance may not meet authentication standards for use as criminal evidence

**6.3** City shall provide written notice to the prosecuting agency in any pending criminal proceeding in which Footage produced during a period of identified non-compliance has been offered or may be offered as evidence.

*Commentary: Section 6.3 is the enforcement mechanism that makes all the others meaningful. If Vendor's non-compliance creates an authentication problem for footage in active prosecutions, City has an obligation — and under this clause a contractual one — to disclose that. Prosecutors who know about the disclosure have their own disclosure obligations to defense counsel under Brady and its progeny.*

## Suggested Additional Provisions

The following provisions go beyond what most cities will be able to negotiate in an initial contract but represent the full standard that makes footage genuinely trustworthy as evidence. They are included here as aspirational language for jurisdictions with significant negotiating leverage or for inclusion in RFP requirements.

**Vendor shall publish Merkle Roots and External Immutable Anchor transaction identifiers to a publicly accessible registry, updated in real time, such that any party — including defense counsel, academic researchers, and the general public — can independently verify the integrity of Footage from Cameras operating under this Agreement without requiring any cooperation from Vendor or City.**

**Vendor shall submit to independent third-party certification of its integrity controls on an annual basis, conducted by a qualified information security firm with no material relationship to Vendor, and shall publish the certification report publicly.**

**Vendor shall provide source code review access for the integrity-critical components of its system — specifically, the hash computation, Merkle chaining, and external anchoring modules — to independent security researchers under a responsible disclosure arrangement.**
