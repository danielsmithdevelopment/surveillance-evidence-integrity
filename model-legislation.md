# Model Legislation: Surveillance Footage Authentication Standards Act

This document provides draft statutory language for state legislatures establishing minimum cryptographic integrity standards as a prerequisite for the admission of surveillance footage as evidence in criminal proceedings. It is designed to be adapted to the specific drafting conventions of the adopting state.

The drafting approach here is intentionally technology-neutral where possible. The statute describes functional requirements — what the integrity controls must be able to demonstrate — rather than mandating specific implementations. This allows the standard to remain valid as technology evolves while still being specific enough to exclude systems that cannot meet the functional requirements.

---

## [STATE] SURVEILLANCE FOOTAGE AUTHENTICATION STANDARDS ACT

### SECTION 1. FINDINGS AND PURPOSE

The Legislature finds and declares:

(a) Mass-deployment surveillance camera networks operated by private vendors under contract with state and local governments are increasingly used as sources of evidence in criminal proceedings, including proceedings that may result in imprisonment or other serious consequences for defendants.

(b) Federal Rule of Evidence 901 and its state equivalents require that evidence be authenticated before admission — that the proponent demonstrate the evidence is what it is claimed to be.

(c) The practical capability to alter digital video footage in a manner that does not leave forensically detectable traces has advanced to the point where it is commercially available to government and intelligence clients. Public reporting based on internal company documents has confirmed that such capabilities exist and are sold as products.

(d) Surveillance camera networks operated by private vendors under government contract cannot currently demonstrate, through independent cryptographic verification, that footage produced from those networks has not been altered between the moment of capture and the moment of introduction in a criminal proceeding.

(e) The absence of cryptographic integrity controls means that authentication of surveillance footage currently depends on the assertion of the producing vendor or agency rather than on independently verifiable mathematical proof.

(f) Defendants in criminal proceedings are entitled to challenge the authenticity of evidence offered against them. The absence of independent cryptographic verification makes meaningful authenticity challenges practically impossible, because there is no technical basis on which to evaluate whether the footage matches what the camera originally recorded.

(g) It is the purpose of this Act to establish minimum technical standards for the cryptographic integrity of surveillance footage that is offered as evidence in criminal proceedings in this state, and to require that the proponent of such footage demonstrate compliance with those standards as a condition of admission.

---

### SECTION 2. DEFINITIONS

As used in this Act:

(a) **"Authentication Record"** means the complete set of cryptographic materials required to verify the integrity of a unit of Covered Footage under Section 4 of this Act, including the hash value computed at capture, the Merkle Chain entries incorporating that hash, the Merkle Root covering the relevant time period, the External Immutable Anchor transaction identifier for that Merkle Root, and any Hardware Attestation records associated with the hash computation.

(b) **"Covered Footage"** means any video, image, or associated metadata captured by a Surveillance Camera System and offered as evidence in a criminal proceeding in this state.

(c) **"Cryptographic Hash"** means the output of a collision-resistant cryptographic hash function applied to a unit of digital data, such that any modification of the data, however small, produces a different hash value with overwhelming probability. SHA-256 or any stronger algorithm approved by the National Institute of Standards and Technology satisfies this definition.

(d) **"External Immutable Anchor"** means a published cryptographic commitment to a Merkle Root stored in a system that: (i) uses content-addressed storage such that the stored value is identified by its own cryptographic hash; (ii) provides permanent storage not subject to modification or deletion by any party, including the operator of the Surveillance Camera System; and (iii) allows independent verification of the stored value by any party without requiring cooperation from the operator of the Surveillance Camera System or the contracting government entity.

(e) **"Hardware Attestation"** means a cryptographic proof, generated by hardware security components within a Surveillance Camera, that the Camera is operating expected firmware and that a specified Cryptographic Hash was computed within that hardware environment at a specified time.

(f) **"Merkle Chain"** means a data structure in which each entry incorporates a Cryptographic Hash of its own content and the hash of the immediately preceding entry, maintained in write-once storage such that no entry can be modified or deleted after it has been appended.

(g) **"Merkle Root"** means the single Cryptographic Hash value representing the complete state of a Merkle Chain at a given point in time, from which the integrity of all entries in the chain can be independently verified through recomputation.

(h) **"Proponent"** means the party offering Covered Footage as evidence in a criminal proceeding, including the prosecution or any law enforcement agency acting on behalf of the prosecution.

(i) **"Surveillance Camera System"** means any network of cameras, sensors, or associated infrastructure operated by or under contract with a state or local government entity for the purpose of capturing vehicle, person, or activity data in public spaces, including without limitation automatic license plate reader systems, traffic monitoring systems, and fixed surveillance camera networks.

---

### SECTION 3. AUTHENTICATION REQUIREMENT

(a) Covered Footage shall not be admitted as evidence in a criminal proceeding in this state unless the Proponent demonstrates, by a preponderance of the evidence, that the Covered Footage meets the authentication standards set forth in Section 4 of this Act.

(b) The demonstration required by subsection (a) shall be made at a pre-trial hearing upon the motion of either party. The court may admit Covered Footage provisionally pending the outcome of a hearing if the court finds that a hearing before trial is not practicable, provided that the Proponent makes the required demonstration no later than the close of the Proponent's case-in-chief.

(c) The authentication requirement of this Section is in addition to, and does not replace, any other applicable authentication requirement under the rules of evidence of this state.

(d) The court shall make findings on the record regarding whether the authentication standard has been met. A finding that the standard has been met or has not been met is reviewable on appeal.

---

### SECTION 4. MINIMUM AUTHENTICATION STANDARDS

To meet the authentication requirement of Section 3, the Proponent shall demonstrate each of the following:

**(a) Hash at Capture.** A Cryptographic Hash of the Covered Footage was computed within the Surveillance Camera that captured the footage, using hardware security components within that Camera, before the footage was transmitted to any network or storage system outside the Camera. The Proponent shall produce the hash value and Hardware Attestation records demonstrating the hardware environment and timing of the computation.

**(b) Merkle Chain Integrity.** The hash computed under subsection (a) is incorporated into a continuous Merkle Chain covering all footage captured by the relevant Surveillance Camera System during the relevant time period. The Proponent shall produce the complete Merkle Chain entries covering the Covered Footage and demonstrate that the chain has not been broken, altered, or interrupted between the entry covering the Covered Footage and the most recent Merkle Root.

**(c) External Immutable Anchoring.** The Merkle Root covering the time period of the Covered Footage has been published to an External Immutable Anchor. The Proponent shall produce the External Immutable Anchor transaction identifier and demonstrate, through independent retrieval from the External Immutable Anchor system, that the Merkle Root value published at that transaction identifier matches the Merkle Root of the chain produced under subsection (b).

**(d) Audit Log Completeness.** A complete Audit Log exists documenting all events associated with the Covered Footage from the moment of capture through the moment of production in the proceeding, including all access, query, export, and transfer events. The Audit Log is maintained in write-once storage and its entries are incorporated in the Merkle Chain required by subsection (b). The Proponent shall produce the complete Audit Log upon the defendant's request.

**(e) Chain Verification.** An independent technical expert, acceptable to the court, has verified that the Cryptographic Hash of the Covered Footage as currently produced matches the hash value incorporated in the Merkle Chain, and that the Merkle Chain verifies against the External Immutable Anchor. The court may waive the independent expert requirement upon agreement of the parties.

---

### SECTION 5. DISCLOSURE OBLIGATIONS

(a) The Proponent shall disclose to the defendant, no later than [30] days before trial or at such earlier time as required by applicable discovery rules, all Authentication Records associated with Covered Footage the Proponent intends to offer.

(b) If the Proponent becomes aware at any time that Covered Footage previously produced may not satisfy the authentication standards of Section 4 — including by reason of a gap in the Merkle Chain, a failure of External Immutable Anchoring, or a discrepancy between the hash of the produced footage and the hash incorporated in the Merkle Chain — the Proponent shall disclose that fact to the defendant and to the court immediately.

(c) Disclosure obligations under this Section are ongoing through the conclusion of any appeal.

---

### SECTION 6. REMEDIES FOR NON-COMPLIANCE

(a) If the court finds that Covered Footage does not meet the authentication standards of Section 4, the footage shall be excluded from evidence.

(b) If the court finds that the Proponent failed to make timely disclosure required by Section 5, the court may, in addition to exclusion of the footage:

- (i) Grant a continuance to allow the defendant adequate time to investigate and respond
- (ii) Give an adverse inference instruction to the jury
- (iii) Impose sanctions on the Proponent as permitted by applicable law

(c) A conviction obtained through the use of Covered Footage that did not meet the authentication standards of Section 4, or that was subject to a disclosure failure under Section 5, may serve as a basis for post-conviction relief under applicable law.

---

### SECTION 7. GOVERNMENT CONTRACTING REQUIREMENTS

(a) Any contract entered into or renewed after the effective date of this Act between a state or local government entity and an operator of a Surveillance Camera System shall require the operator to implement and maintain the technical controls necessary to generate Authentication Records meeting the standards of Section 4.

(b) Any contract subject to subsection (a) shall include a requirement that the operator provide Authentication Records to defense counsel in any criminal proceeding in which Covered Footage from the system is offered as evidence, upon receipt of a valid legal process or written authorization from the prosecuting agency.

(c) Any contract subject to subsection (a) shall prohibit the operator from integrating with, providing data access to, or sharing footage with any system or third party capable of altering footage in a manner that does not produce a detectable, permanent record in the Merkle-chained Audit Log required by Section 4(d).

(d) A government entity that enters into a contract not meeting the requirements of this Section after the effective date of this Act shall not offer Covered Footage from the subject Surveillance Camera System as evidence in any criminal proceeding in this state.

---

### SECTION 8. APPLICABILITY AND TRANSITION

(a) This Act applies to any criminal proceeding in which Covered Footage is offered as evidence on or after [DATE], regardless of when the footage was captured or when the relevant contract was entered into.

(b) In any proceeding to which this Act applies, the Proponent may satisfy the authentication requirement of Section 4 with respect to footage captured before the effective date of this Act by demonstrating, at minimum, subsections (b), (c), and (d) of Section 4 through retroactive Merkle chaining and external anchoring of existing footage, provided that:

- (i) The retroactive process is performed by an independent technical expert
- (ii) The expert provides testimony that, to the best of their knowledge, the footage was not altered prior to the retroactive process
- (iii) The court finds the retroactive process sufficient to establish the reliability of the footage

(c) With respect to footage captured after [DATE + 18 MONTHS], only footage meeting all requirements of Section 4 at the time of capture shall be admissible.

---

### SECTION 9. CONSTRUCTION

(a) This Act shall be construed broadly to protect the right of defendants to challenge the authenticity of digital evidence offered against them.

(b) Nothing in this Act limits the right of a defendant to challenge the authenticity of Covered Footage on grounds independent of the standards set forth in Section 4, including through expert testimony regarding the technical capability to alter footage of the relevant type.

(c) Nothing in this Act shall be construed to create an inference that footage meeting the standards of Section 4 has not been altered. Meeting the authentication standard is a floor, not a ceiling.

---

### SECTION 10. EFFECTIVE DATE

This Act takes effect [DATE].

## Drafting Notes

**On the transition provision:** Section 8 acknowledges that requiring all historical footage to meet the full standard immediately would effectively bar use of any existing footage as evidence, since no current system generates Authentication Records. The transition gives prosecutors 18 months to work with vendors on retroactive Merkle chaining of existing footage, while setting a hard cutoff date after which only footage captured with the full controls in place is admissible. States with larger active caseloads involving Flock footage may want a longer transition period.

**On the independent expert requirement:** Section 4(e) requires an independent technical expert to verify the chain. Courts will need a process for certifying such experts. State public defender offices may want to develop relationships with qualified experts in advance of this Act taking effect, since the prosecution will have an institutional advantage in expert access.

**On federal proceedings:** This Act applies to state criminal proceedings only. Federal proceedings are governed by the Federal Rules of Evidence. A parallel effort to amend FRE 901 to reflect these standards, or to develop standing orders in individual federal districts, would be necessary to cover federal prosecutions involving Flock footage.

**On the External Immutable Anchor definition:** The definition in Section 2(d) is written to capture Arweave and functionally equivalent systems without naming specific technologies. If a state legislature prefers to be more specific, "including without limitation the Arweave permanent storage network or any successor system providing equivalent permanence and independent verifiability" can be added to the definition.
